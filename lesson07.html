Learning Objectives

Identity is the New Perimeter

In the post-perimeter era, controlling who can access what — and when — is the cornerstone of modern security architecture. This lesson maps directly to CompTIA Security+ Domain 4.0: Identity and Access Management.

By the End of This Lesson, You Will Be Able To:
  • 1Define and differentiate the AAA security framework components
  • 2Compare authentication factors and explain why MFA is essential
  • 3Contrast SSO with federated identity and identify use cases for each
  • 4Apply Least Privilege and RBAC principles to real-world access scenarios
  • 5Execute the Joiner-Mover-Leaver lifecycle framework to prevent orphaned accounts and privilege creep
  • 6Explain the role of PAM in protecting high-value administrative credentials

Key Terms — 6 Must-Know Concepts
AAA
Authentication, Authorization, Accounting — the three pillars of access control
MFA
Multi-Factor Authentication — two or more distinct factor categories required
SSO
Single Sign-On — one credential unlocks multiple systems within a trust boundary
RBAC
Role-Based Access Control — permissions assigned to roles, not individuals
PAM
Privileged Access Management — vaulting and auditing elevated administrative credentials
Deprovisioning
Systematic revocation of all access rights when a user exits or transfers
🧩 Opening Scenario — The Joiner-Mover-Leaver Problem

A hospital network administrator notices that Dr. Chen — who transferred from Oncology to the Emergency Department six months ago — still has full access to the oncology patient database, chemo medication orders, and the departmental budget system. Last week, she was assigned ED access on top of her existing rights. Meanwhile, a former IT contractor who left the organization in January still has an active VPN account.

Discussion prompt: What security principles were violated? What could go wrong? What controls should have prevented this?

🟢 JoinerNew hire provisioned with role-appropriate access
🟡 MoverTransfer creates accumulated permission sprawl
🔴 LeaverDeparture without deprovisioning = orphaned account

Authentication (proving identity), Authorization (granting rights), Accounting (logging activity). Walk through RADIUS and TACACS+ as real-world AAA implementations. Hotel key card analogy. Formative check: students identify A, AA, or AAA in 3 example scenarios.

The five factor types (Something you know/have/are/do/somewhere you are). Attack resistance by factor. Live demo: Google Authenticator TOTP. Show how SIM-swapping defeats SMS-based MFA. Students evaluate factor combinations for a banking app vs. a physical server room.

Direct Instruction

Module 3 — SSO, Federation & Identity Providers

SSO within a trust boundary (Active Directory) vs. federation across organizational boundaries (SAML, OAuth, OIDC). IdP vs. SP roles. Real-world example: "Login with Google." Airport passport control analogy for federation. When federation is appropriate vs. when it creates risk.

Core IAM Concepts & Security+ Mappings

Use these cards as a study reference. Each concept includes the Security+ exam framing, a real-world analogy, and the key distinctions examiners love to test.

🔑
Objective 4.1

AAA Framework

The three-pillar model underpinning every enterprise access system.

  • Authentication — Prove who you are (credentials, biometrics)
  • Authorization — Determine what you're allowed to do
  • Accounting — Record what you actually did (logs, audit trails)
Real-World AnalogyHotel check-in: showing your ID (Authentication) → getting a room-specific key card (Authorization) → the hotel recording your entry/exit times (Accounting)
🔐
Objective 4.2

Authentication Factors & MFA

MFA requires two or more factor categories — not just two passwords.

  • Type 1 — Something you know (password, PIN, passphrase)
  • Type 2 — Something you have (TOTP token, smart card, FIDO2 key)
  • Type 3 — Something you are (fingerprint, iris scan, voice)
  • Type 4 — Something you do (gait, keystroke dynamics)
  • Type 5 — Somewhere you are (geolocation, network context)
Exam TrapA password + a PIN = same factor type (both Type 1). This is NOT MFA. MFA requires different categories.
🌐
Objective 4.3

SSO vs. Identity Federation

Both reduce password fatigue — but they operate at different trust boundaries.

Feature SSO Federation
Scope Single org/domain Cross-org / cloud
Protocol Kerberos, LDAP SAML, OAuth, OIDC
Risk Single point of failure Trust chain attack
Real-World AnalogySSO = an office master key for all doors in your building. Federation = TSA PreCheck trusted across all participating airlines (different organizations).
🛡️
Objective 4.4

Least Privilege & RBAC

Limit the blast radius of any account compromise to only what that user legitimately needs.

  • Least Privilege — Minimum permissions to perform the job function
  • Need-to-Know — Access only to information relevant to current duties
  • RBAC — Assign permissions to roles; assign users to roles
  • Privilege Creep — Accumulated rights over time without periodic review
  • Access Review — Periodic audit to recertify or revoke permissions
Real-World AnalogyA hospital clerk needs access to billing records — not surgical scheduling or medication dispensing. RBAC ensures the "Billing Clerk" role can't order opioids.
👑
Objective 4.5

Privileged Access Management (PAM)

Admin and service accounts represent the highest-value targets in any environment. PAM treats them accordingly.

  • Credential Vaulting — Store privileged passwords in an encrypted vault
  • Just-in-Time Access — Elevate only when needed; auto-expire afterward
  • Session Recording — Full video/keylog of privileged sessions
  • Credential Rotation — Auto-rotate passwords after each use
Real-World AnalogyA bank vault key that only activates for a 15-minute window, requires two people to use, and automatically video-records every second it's in the lock.
🔚
Lifecycle Management

Joiner-Mover-Leaver & Deprovisioning

Identity governance must follow employees through every lifecycle phase.

  • Joiner — Provision with role-appropriate rights only; use IdP onboarding workflow
  • Mover — Remove old role's access before or simultaneously with adding new role
  • Leaver — Immediately disable account; revoke tokens; archive data; audit log
  • Orphaned Account — Active account with no legitimate owner; a standing attack surface
Exam TipCompTIA frequently tests the order of operations for offboarding. Disabling the account comes before deletion; account review before disabling is best practice.
